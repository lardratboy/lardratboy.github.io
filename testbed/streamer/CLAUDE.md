# ifs-testbed-discussion

The IFS tower contact sheet and the design discussion around turning it into a streaming voxel engine. `ifsandwhats.md` is the living design doc; its §11 tracks every open question.

- `sol-v3-tower-contact-sheet.html`: the **base-3** testbed. **Do not edit it.** ifsandwhats.md cites its line numbers.
- `sol-v4-tower-contact-sheet.html`: the **radix-4** port (two binary levels per level, Morton digits). This is the working page.
- `walk-core.js`: pure, no imports: **walking**, Minecraft's way (docs/dungeon-log.md §11, asked for 2026-11-14; flying stays the default). A box 1.5 × 4.5 × 1.5 voxels, the eye 4 up, **dyadic on purpose** (a box clamped at L sits at L − 0.75, which adds back to L exactly, so a walker on a floor stands at an integer). `stepWalk(w, world, { move, sprint, jump }, dt)`: the wish eased in, jumps from the ground only, exact gravity, substeps of a quarter voxel axis by axis (y first), each stopped flush at the first solid layer; a stopped move on the ground tries a voxel higher (stairs), and off an edge it snaps a voxel down; `lift` eases the eye. **Climbing** (`climb`, held; the page hands it the run, `climb: sprint`, since Alt-W and Ctrl-W close tabs; docs/dungeon-log.md §11.4): pushing into a wall *beside* the box (its face exactly on a voxel face, the layer beyond solid) goes up it at `WALK.climb` (× the run's), no gravity; a climb that stops pushing hangs, and a hang only follows a climb (`climbOf`), so running along a wall off a ledge still falls. `settle` (up out of rock), `wishOf` (the page's fwd and right), `lookBy` (first-person look). The world is a function `world(lo, hi)`: the page's is `openWorld(withHoles(dagWorld(...).solid, secretDoors(plan)), side)` (outside the world air beside and above, solid below; holes the walker passes though the DAG is solid).
- `dag-core.js`: a pure ES module (no DOM, no imports): hash-consed binary voxel DAG, Oh-labelled classes (SSVDAG), census. The seed of ifsandwhats.md §8.5 step 1. `isFull(store, counts, id, level)` is the only way to ask whether a node is completely full (a count can't tell past level 17). `prune(keys, roots)` keeps only what the roots reach (graft leaves the pieces it unioned behind); `pruneWorld(store, root)` is that as a fresh store, for the scatter and lattice builds. `storeView({ keys })` is a read-only store over a snapshot's keys (the chunk workers', and the page's worker-built worlds), which `grow`s by a store's `keysFrom(id)`, an edit's new nodes. `importSame(store, keys, from, to)` makes a slice of such keys real, each node checked to land on its own id (−1, or the node held twice; gizmo-log §14).
- `chunk-core.js`: a pure ES module (imports only `dag-core.js`): `expandChunk` turns one 64³ chunk of a DAG at a given lod into 4-byte instance records (Morton cell, face mask, 8³-brick Oh pose). ifsandwhats.md §8.5 step 3. Its `lodRule` option (step 3b, Q6) picks which coarse cells are drawn, from `createCounts` voxel counts. The default `'any'` is step 3's behaviour. `'adaptive'` was Q6's decided rule for coarse lods and is still the streamer's default (`lodRuleOf`), **but the stream viewer draws with `'shadow'` by default since 2026-11-10** (`'any'` for a day before it, `?rule=any`; `?rule=adaptive` for the old one), at detail 4 (the streamer's default is 2; `?detail=2`): adaptive opens see-through holes in one-voxel content (docs/lodacne.md). **`'shadow'`** (2026-11-10) is adaptive plus the best cell of every column of each 8³ brick along x, y and z (`addShadow`, which job-core's masks call too), so no axis-aligned hole opens. Its slab coverage is any's, exactly (lodacne.md §9). It reports `quads` and `openQuads`, the faces shown and those each open side adds, for `quadsShown`, so the viewer can count triangles without its records. Its `colour` option (step 6) adds `attrs`, one word per record (seam tail included): the field at a voxel, or the count-weighted mean under a coarse cell, descending `meanDepth` levels (`cellAttr`). A field with a `mean` of its own (a baked one) is asked that instead, at every lod, lod 0 included; every named field walks as before.
- `job-core.js`: pure (imports `dag-core.js`, `chunk-core.js`): **the job, faster** (computedags.md §9 step 1, docs/job-speed-log.md). `fastChunk` gives `expandChunk`'s bytes with seams (records, words, tail, quads, occupied; not `keptVoxels`): a full node of side ≥ 4 fills its grid block and lists only its shell, a per-brick rule's kept cells are a 512-bit mask per (rule, lod, node) in `createJobCache`, and the seam test is cached per job and walks from the neighbour chunk. `createJobRunner` runs records jobs through it with one cache for the snapshot's life; pages still use `expandChunk`. 4–12× without colour; with colour, colour is most of the job.
- `baked-core.js`: pure (imports `dag-core.js`, `chunk-core.js`'s `CHUNK_LEVELS`): **a baked colour field** (docs/glb-colour-plan.md step 2). `createBaked(store, skin, D, words)` reads an asset's words (rgb8, one per skin voxel in rank order, the skin's Morton order; the high byte ignored) as a colour field: `field(x, y, z)` is the voxel's word by a rank walk, `field.mean(level, x, y, z, out)` the exact mean of the cell's skin as floats (a rank range summed by block sums every 64 words, at most 126 words scanned). A cell with no skin (the seam tail, a coarse cell the adaptive rule draws inside) takes its smallest ancestor's mean within `climb` levels (6: its own chunk, so the stamp rule holds), else the whole skin's; `stats` counts both. A walk starts where the last one can be trusted (the path memo in `descend`, glb-colour-log §6: the deepest level two points share, by the xor's top bit, never below where the last walk stopped), so a skin voxel in Morton order reads about one child, not D. The job draws it through `cellAttr`'s `mean` hook (step 3, docs/glb-colour-log.md §3), in the workers since step 5 (§7).
- `chunk-glsl.js`: `GLSL_DECODE`, the record decode every shader prepends (moved out of chunk-viewer in step 4), and `GLSL_ATTR`, the attribute word's decode (step 6).
- `attr-core.js`: pure, no imports: **Q13's attribute word** (step 6): 32 bits, RGBA8 bytes, colour in the high 6 bits of R, G and B, the rest reserved (written 0, ignored on read). `packAttr`, `attrRGB`, `attrOf8`.
- `colours.js`: the colour field each world names. A snapshot carries a descriptor (`{ kind: 'shape' | 'row', … }`), and `colourOf(desc, world)` turns it into a field on whichever side asks (workers can't receive functions). Shape colours live beside the shapes in `experiments/shapes.mjs`. A baked descriptor (`{ kind: 'baked', skin, words, bits }`) is data, not a name: `createJobRunner` passes its own `{ store, counts, D }` as `world`, and `colourOf` builds `createBaked` over it (throwing without one).
- `chunk-viewer.html`: draws those records as instanced cubes in raw WebGL2, decoding the centre in the vertex shader. `?selftest` runs its decode probe.
- `stream-core.js`: a pure ES module (imports `dag-core.js`, `chunk-core.js`, `ray-core.js`, `edit-core.js` for the colour table, `scene-core.js` for the scene table, and `log-core.js` for the one log's): the **chunk virtualiser** of ifsandwhats.md §8.5 step 4. `selectChunks` (the distance-refined cut of 64³ chunks), `coverOf` (what to draw from what is resident), `createStreamer` (job order, admission under budgets, LRU eviction), `createJobRunner` (one `expandChunk` on a `worldSnapshot`), `clearance`. §2b, `chooseKinds`: **raster or ray per chunk, by density** (cubes per footprint pixel, with hysteresis; docs/ray-depth-plan.md), against a threshold linear in the chunk's occupancy (`rayDensity` empty → `rayDensityFull` full, fitted to the real-GPU benches; docs/path-occupancy.md). `recordCount` is the cubes drawn, seam tail left out. A chunk resident in the wrong kind is drawn until its replacement arrives; nothing flushes the cache. **Edits** (docs/edit-stream-log.md): `setRoot(root, { paint })` opens a transaction, held results that all install in one frame (the flip), so every frame draws one root; the flip installs every held result the cut wants **or its plan draws** (a chunk held as a leaf and merged by a jump is drawn as its absent parent's fallback child; docs/onelog-log.md §5); `stampOf` (the seven nodes a job reads) and `createPaint` (a colour revision per chunk) say whether an entry is still right; `createTailer` and `runner.tail` carry new nodes and colour ops to a worker, a scene log's drops (`scene`), or the one log's delta (`ops`, `oneLog`; docs/onelog-log.md §2), which takes blocks and drops in one world. A world follows one road: the runner refuses a block log's ops with a scene log's, and either with the one log. `setStore(store)` swaps a world's view for a real store holding the same ids, with no flush (gizmo-log §14). `createJobRunner` asks `colourOf(desc, { store, counts, D })` with its own view, which a baked field needs. `stream-pool.js`'s `tail` and `chunk-worker.js` carry them (edit mode, docs/edit-mode-log.md).
- `stream-pool.js` + `chunk-worker.js`: the browser threads under a streamer (block-showroom's `GenerationPool`, minus every decision). The pool keeps every tail of the world, so a restarted worker or a late main-thread runner gets the snapshot and all of them, in order.
- `ray-core.js` (pure) + `ray-glsl.js`: the **ray path** (§8.5 step 5), off by default in the viewer: raster only again since 2026-11-09, as it felt snappier than by density (`?ray=density`, `?ray=<lod>`). Chunks at lod ≥ `rayLod` are pages (8³ brick grid + deduplicated 8³ bricks); `GLSL_RAY` marches the cover tree. `classifyPagesCPU` / `traceCPU` are the CPU twins, reading the textures' exact layout. Both tracers take a `tMax`: the viewer draws the raster offscreen with a depth texture, and each ray stops at the raster's depth (`GLSL_RAY_BOUND` in the viewer; the canvas has no MSAA). The ray pass runs only on the pixels a stencil marks: the traced chunks' boxes, depth-tested by hand against the raster (`markTraced`, docs/stencil-pass.md). Step 6 adds each page's attribute block (per brick: a 16-word shown mask, then a word per shown cell) in a global heap (`createHeap`), found through a `pageAttr` texture; `attrAtCPU` / `GLSL_RAY_ATTR` read a hit's word.
- `stream-viewer.html`: flies through big worlds, opening on the D = 8 kit when the link names no world (`kit:1`, `KIT_OPENING`; row 142 before 2026-11-14) (the mouse orbits, pans and dollies as sol-v4's, `camera-core.js`; WASD flies) (testbed rows to L = 8, picked in a panel the world menu opens, since 194 options buried the rest (`openRowPicker`, docs/world-picker.md), shapes to D = 12, the scatter and lattice worlds, the kit to D = 16, the caves to D = 12, clay and flat ground to D = 20, the dungeon to D = 12, the city to D = 13) through the streamer. `?selftest` runs `streamSelftest()`. The lattice has controls of its own (R, size to 128, gap, turns, axes, pitch; `?lattice=R,nx,ny,nz,gap,turns,axes,align`, 7-field links read as align 0). So has the kit (libraries, ornaments, ground, colour frame; `?kit=D,libs,ornaments,ground,colourLevel`, libs dot-joined), rebuilt live with the eye kept. The four seeded worlds (scatter, lattice, kit, caves; the dungeon too) have a seed control (`setSeed`: the box, −/+, random; keys `[` `]` `R`), which rewrites the world id (`kit:2`) and reloads, debounced, keeping the eye except in the caves (docs/world-seed.md). Every `loadWorld` supersedes the one in flight: the last world asked for wins, and the toast shows build progress. Hidden raster chunks can be skipped (`queryRunner` or `depthRunner`, `occlusion-core.js`; queries by default in the caves and the kit, off elsewhere, `?occlusion=off|queries|depth`). Both panels, the numbers and the controls, are hidden by default: a button at the top right or `H` shows them, `?hud=1` opens with them, and with them off the lower right corner shows the frame rate (docs/hud-toggle.md). The keys and the link's options are a dialog (`#helpModal`): `keys (?)` at the top right or `?` opens it, ×, Esc, `?` or a press around it closes it (docs/hud-toggle.md). A world with no colour (a file of a voxelized asset) opens in the gamut colour mode, one with colour in attribute, until a mode is picked (`colourForWorld`); a baked file draws in its own colours (glb-colour step 5). **Walking** (docs/dungeon-log.md §11): `C`, a button or `?walk=1` walks (walk-core.js; `walk`, `walkWorld`, `walkStep`, `setWalk`), on the log's root, the dungeon's secret doors passable; WASD on the ground, space jumps, shift runs and climbs (run at a wall to go up it, stop pushing to hang), a drag looks instead of orbiting, a click locks the pointer to look (not in edit mode, whose tools keep their free cursor), the wheel doesn't dolly; the walker follows an eye moved by anything else. `T` or a button draws the wireframe, with a triangle count in the lower left (`?wire=1`, `wireSelftest()`, docs/wireframe.md). **Three faces a cube** (docs/city-frame-cost.md §8, 2026-11-18): `rasterVS` draws of each axis's two faces the one on the eye's side (18 indices, `RASTER_DRAW`), which halved the raster pass (it's bound by geometry, not pixels); `facesSelftest()` holds every frame to the six-face reference (`rasterVS('', true)`), byte for byte. **SSAO** (a prototype, off by default; docs/vdagssao.md): `Y`, a button or `?ssao=1` (`?ssao=slices,steps,scale,radius`; `?ssaoshow=1` the AO alone) darkens the frame by GTAO on the raster's depth, with normals rebuilt from depth and snapped to an axis, in two passes after `renderView` (`drawSSAO`), so no frame a test compares changes; the blur keeps a face's own texels (same normal, same plane). Traced chunks write no depth, so they get none. `ssaoBench()` or `?ssaobench` times each setting against none (the wall clock, off and on interleaved, medians). **Actors** (docs/actors-log.md steps 2 and 3): `?actors=N` or the panel puts N fliers in any world, ticked by actor-core.js on the main thread at a fixed 60 Hz in `stepActors`, leashed to the eye and kept SPACE = 16 voxels off it (step 3b), and drawn as their bodies (body-core.js: one draw an actor, the turn in `BODY_VS`) by `drawActors` into the raster target after the occlusion queries and before the ray pass; the near plane takes the nearest flier into account (`actorClearance`). A new root on the same store (an edit, an undo) rescues the fliers it trapped (`actorWorld`: `diffBox`, then `rescue`). `actorSelftest()`. **Edit mode** (docs/edit-mode-log.md, `B`, clay, flat ground, the dungeon, the kit, the shapes, the rows and files of them): **one log** a world (log-core's `createOneLog`, docs/onelog-log.md §3), so `Z` / shift-`Z` undo and redo the latest op of either kind, and an undone drop is picked up into its box (in the world tool). `W.edit.kind` is the tool in hand ('blocks' or 'scene'), `W.edit.tools` the world's: **clay, flat ground and the dungeon have both, `Tab` between them** (`setTool`, onelog-plan Q1), everything else the world tool alone. **Both tools have the free cursor and the orbit camera** (onelog-plan Q3; no pointer lock or crosshair since 2026-11-12): the pointer aims, a click acts on release, a drag is the camera. Blocks: an outline; left breaks, right places, middle takes the colour under the pointer (blocks and drops, through a `createOneTable`), a button held still 200 ms digs at the repeat's rate with the pointer aiming and the camera kept, wheel or `-` `=` for the block side, `1`–`8` for colour. `editSelftest()`, `mixedSelftest()` (blocks and drops mixed on flat ground); `editBench()` or `?editbench` for what a click costs. **The world tool** (kind 'scene', docs/scene-log.md §4, docs/gizmo-log.md; `takesWorlds`, gizmo-plan step 4; on clay and flat ground too): the kit is imported into a real store when it's installed (the identity, checked), and so is a file of at most `tool.importMax` (2^19) nodes; a shape's or a row's store is real already. A file with baked colour, with none, or holding a node twice takes none: `W.noEdit` is why, and `B` says it. **The scatter, the lattice, the caves and a file over that size are made real at the first `B`** (`makeReal`, gizmo-plan step 5, docs/gizmo-log.md §14): `importSame` in slices of `tool.slice` with the toast, then stream-core's `setStore`, then the world tool. `toolSelftest()`. A drag off the handles is the mouse camera (docs/camera.md). A ghost box follows the pointer (scene-core's `dropAt` against the hit) with marks for the child's underside and front; **a click opens the placement box** for the shelf world the "place" picker or `1`–`8` names, and `Enter` bakes it (one `log.drop`), `Esc` closes it, shift-click drops at once. On the box: arrows move it (16 a step, or 1), rings turn it by quarters, corners double or halve it, the body slides it over the world; any other drag, or a right drag, looks. `J L` spin, `I K` tip, `U O` roll (as you see them, from the camera's heading), `M` mirrors, `V` is "underside to the face", wheel or `-` `=` the scale (¼ to ×4, as it fits), `G` the snap (16, relative to the first live copy, or the voxel), `Q` / `E` fly. `Z` or a right click undoes the last op, and a drop **opens its box where it stood** (a box a click opened is only closed). The turn is any of dag-core's 48. `Tab` is the block tool on clay, and says blocks are clay's elsewhere. **The child is drawn in the box and in the ghost** (`drawPreview`, docs/gizmo-log.md §9; `P` or `?preview=0` turns it off): its records at a lod, turned by uniforms through `previewMap`, sorted in the canvas's own depth buffer, solid in front of the raster's depth and faint behind it. **Which lod is gizmo-core's `previewPlan`** (gizmo-log §11): the coarse one (`previewLod`, one chunk) at once; while the box is still, finer lods made 6 ms a frame (`previewMake`, only the chunks `previewChunks` says can show something) down to the voxels a bake makes, each drawn when it's whole; while it moves, a handle is held or the camera moves, the finest lod of at most 2^18 cubes. shift-`-` and shift-`=` step where it rests (`detailKey`). Every decision about the box is `gizmo-core.js`'s. `sceneSelftest()`, `previewSelftest()` (`test/browser/scene.test.js`).
- `vdag-core.js`: pure (imports only `dag-core.js`; colour fields are handed in): **world files** (docs/binaryoutput.md §4, §11). `writeVdag(worlds)` / `readVdag(bytes)`: one or more whole worlds in a tagged-section file, sharing nodes, with each node written where it's first used (`encodeGraph`, `decodeGraph`), each world's descriptor, table, view and source, and a print of its colour field (`colourPrint`, `resolveColour`). A baked world (`{ kind: 'baked', skin, words, bits }`, the skin's nodes in its keys) adds an `ATTR` section: the skin as a payload that goes on from NODE's level tables (`graphEncoder` / `graphDecoder`'s `aside`, so each ATTR depends on NODE alone), and the words gzipped at eight bits a channel or six (`bits: 6`, word encoding 2: `to6` stored, widened on read, so lod-0 words don't move and coarse ones move a step at most; docs/glb-colour-log.md §5); its print is of its own words (`bakedPrint`), and a mismatch is damage (§4). The reader treats a file as outside input. `vdag-worker.js` runs both off the main thread. The viewer's shelf (`shelf`, ids `file:<n>`) holds worlds as data; a link can't name one, so `saveView` writes what it was built from (`sourceOf`).
- `camera-core.js`: pure, no imports: **the mouse camera**, sol-v4's (docs/camera.md, 2026-11-12). Left drag orbits, right drag or shift and left pans, middle drag and the wheel dolly (eased, `easeStep`), two fingers pinch and pan (`modeOf`, `pinchOf`, `wheelLog`). Not a second camera: `orbit`, `pan` and `dolly` take the page's `{ eye, yaw, pitch }` and a radius (the target is that far along the view) and give the camera back. `dolly` pushes the target on past a threshold (sol-v4's push-through), so the wheel goes through walls. The page (`orbitRadius`, `cameraBy`, `cameraEase`) picks the radius down the middle of the view at the first gesture after anything else moved the camera, and keeps the push-through threshold from then (half a second of the fly keys). The wheel was the fly speed; that's alt-wheel now.
- `pad-core.js`: pure, no imports: **the gamepad**, read as the stream viewer's fly camera wants it (standard mapping; radial dead zone, square response; button presses as edges). The page polls `navigator.getGamepads()` once a frame in `move()` and only applies what `readPad` returns. docs/gamepad.md.
- `occlusion-core.js`: pure, no imports: **occlusion culling's decisions** (docs/occlusion.md): queries by default in the caves and the kit, off elsewhere (`occlusionDefault`, from the Safari benches, §6–8 there). After the raster pass the viewer asks whether each raster chunk's box (grown by a cell) lies behind the frame's depth, by occlusion query (`?occlusion=queries`) or against the depth read back as 16² tiles of farthest depth (`?occlusion=depth`, `hiddenByTiles`). `createOcclusion` says which chunks to skip and which boxes to ask about: no result, drawn; a result belongs to its entry and its stay in the list; one query in flight per chunk; hidden chunks re-asked at once, visible ones every `revisit` frames; a missed page frame forgets everything. `nearEye`: boxes the near plane may cut are always drawn. Not a streamer decision: stream-core doesn't know.
- `actor-core.js`: pure (imports only `dag-core.js`): **actors** (docs/actors.md §9 step 1, docs/actors-log.md). `dagWorld(store, root, D)` is the world as an actor asks it (`solid(lo, hi)`: anything in this box of voxels? outside counts as solid; `room`, the largest aligned EMPTY node round a box). `createActors(N)` is a population as arrays (`alive`, `size` per actor per axis, `pos` / `vel` int32 in 1/256 voxel, the wander's `epoch` / `until`, last tick's `old`, the `room` cache, the clock `t`). `tickFliers` moves everyone one tick: a wander easing toward a hashed goal, a box leash to the eye (`leash.at`) with a personal space inside it (`leash.inner`, `spaceOf`: a force away from the eye, so they keep near but never on it; spawns avoid it given `away`), soft pushes from `buildGrid` / `pushOf`, then a swept box one axis at a time, the longest clear move by bisection. `spawnNear` and `regather` (both from the world's nearest voxel to the eye, `inside`; with `trap`, never into a sealed pocket), `advanceFliers` (the fast forward), `restHits`. **Trapped** (actors-log.md step 3): `trapped` is 'rock' (a block over it) or 'sealed' (`sealedAt`: a flood of air from its box never reaches the outer layer of the box grown by `reach`, 32 in the viewer; two exact shortcuts first); `diffBox` is the bounding box where two roots differ; `rescue` spawns every trapped actor near where it was, asking only those within reach of the change. D ≤ 22.
- `body-core.js`: pure (imports dag-core, chunk-core, specimen-core, lattice-core's `stackOf`, scatter-core's `PROPER`, attr-core, actor-core's `mix`): **actors' bodies** (docs/actors-log.md step 3). A library of 64 block-showroom specimens, R = 3 + k % 9, each the first recipe of its run that fills its R on every axis and is one piece (`bodyOf`, `goodBody`; 8–26% pass). `turnMap(e, R)` is `rotateOcc`'s map on points, `turnedBox` the actor's box, `bodyRecords` the records and words `expandChunk` makes of the body padded into 16³ (once a body; the shader turns them), `actorBody(seed, i)` slot i's body and turn. Turns are fixed at spawn.
- `scene-core.js`: pure (imports `dag-core.js`, `actor-core.js` and attr-core's `rgb8`): **worlds placed in a world** (docs/scenegraph.md §11, docs/scene-plan.md, docs/scene-log.md), promoted from experiments/scene-sketch.mjs. A placement is `{ world: { store, root, D, skin? }, at, turn, scale }`: the child's cube corner in whole parent voxels (it may hang off the edge), one of dag-core's 48 Oh elements (the viewer offers the 24 proper ones), and 2^scale parent voxels a child voxel (down by the *any* rule). `bakeScene(store, root, D, placements)` unions each into the parent's DAG (the store must intern: import a storeView's keys into a `createStore` first). It shares every node the offset doesn't cut, and memoizes the import (`importWorld`, per parent store, child store and root), the preparation (`prepared`, per root, scale and turn) and the top-down placement (per T, level and corner relative to the child; `PLACE_MEMO`: levels below `floor` 2 aren't kept, and at `max` 2²² entries it's emptied, since a Map holds 2²⁴ and one D = 10 knight was 5.6 million with no floor; `placeMemoSize`, scene-log §5.3). `compositeWorld(parent, placements)` answers actor-core's solid, room and fill with each child asked in its own frame (only the root's edge is solid). The two are held to each other and to a dense stamp. `sceneField(store, base, list)` (step 2, docs/scene-log.md §2) colours a baked scene: a cell takes the latest placement solid in it (bounds, then `boxHits` on the prepared child T), asked for the child cell of level l − scale holding the cell's centre, back through the turn (a baked child's exact `mean` over the parent's store, a named one at the cell's centre), else the base. It needs the sample's level, which chunk-core's `meanUnder` passes as a fourth argument since step 2. It keeps the placements meeting the sample's 64-voxel block (16 placements: 1.15–1.5× the kit's colour alone over the whole world). It has no `mean`, refuses a parent with one, and hands the base back for an empty list. **The scene log** (step 3, scene-log §3): `createSceneLog(base)` is createEditLog's shape for drops (`drop(store, D, p)` → null or `{ paint }`, the child's bounds in level-6 blocks; undo, redo, an epoch, `delta(epoch, seen, seenWorlds)`), seven int32 words a drop with T, the prepared child's id, since a worker can't intern; a drop into rock is an op (it recolours), one with no voxel in the world isn't. Worlds are registered once, a baked skin rewritten to the parent's id, and cross to a follower once. `createSceneTable(store, fieldOf)` is the worker's copy, `sync` by createBlockTable's rules, `field(base)` the scene field. **Where a drop lands** (step 4, scene-log §4): `dropAt(hit, box, { grid, ref })` puts the placed child's content box against the hit face from the open side, centred on the hit voxel (half rounded up), and with `ref` snaps the two axes along the face to ref + 16k; `snapRef` is the first live copy of the same (world, turn, scale), so undoing it hands the grid on; `Y_TURNS` = [0, 41, 5, 44], the four turns about y. `test/scene-drop.test.js` holds them, with 7 mutants.
- `gizmo-core.js`: pure (imports dag-core's `MUL`, `INV`, `applyG` only): **the placement box's decisions** (docs/gizmo-plan.md, docs/gizmo-log.md). A state is `{ c2, turn, scale }`: `c2` the **pivot, doubled** (three integers; the corner follows from it by `placementOf`, the box's centre on the pivot or half a voxel above it), a turn of the 48, a scale. ## Rules

- **`classify()` (GLSL) and `classifyCPU()` change together, line for line.** The same goes for `solidExtent` / `solidExtentCPU`, `classifyBricks` / the brick compiler, and `bindRow` / the pack functions. Run both suites after any change. `docs/lockstep-verification.md` explains why lockstep alone is not enough: a bug both sides share passes it.
- **Digit order is Morton, octant-major**, decided in `digitOf` / `DIGIT_Q` / `centreOf` (JS) and `digitOf` (GLSL) only. Never compute a digit any other way.
- **64-bit masks are `[lo, hi]` uint32 pairs**; rule stacks are `Uint32Array(2*MAXL)`. End every write with `>>> 0`.
- A new kind of row needs an **independent** count or geometry check in `core.test.js`, not just lockstep. The same goes for a new `census()` count in `dag-core.test.js`.
- **A record's layout** (bits 0–17 Morton cell, 18–23 mask, 24–29 pose) is decided in `chunk-core.js` (`packRecord`, `mortonOf`, `shownMask`) and decoded in `chunk-glsl.js`'s `GLSL_DECODE` only. Both viewers and the probe prepend `GLSL_DECODE`; change the two sides together and run `chunk.test.js`.
- **`stream-core.js` stays pure** (no DOM, no gl, no Worker): every decision the streamer makes is tested in Node. `stream-pool.js` and the page only move messages and buffers. A new streamer decision needs a frame invariant in `frameChecker` (`test/stream-core.test.js`) and the same check in the page's `invariants()`.
- **`classifyPages` / `traceRay` (`ray-glsl.js`) and `classifyPagesCPU` / `traceCPU` (`ray-core.js`) change together, line for line**, and the CPU mirrors in the viewer stay word for word what the textures hold. Run `ray.test.js` after any change.
- **The path rule (`chooseKinds`, stream-core §2b) is written three times**: in `stream-core.js`, in `expectedKinds` (`test/stream-core.test.js`'s `frameChecker`) and in the page's `invariants()`. Change all three together. The same goes for `rasterTMax` (GLSL, shared by the ray pass and `markTraced`'s box shader) and its float64 copies in `boundProbe` and `maskProbe`.
- **Brick dedup rests on CLAUDE.md's lod-rule rule**: a brick's cells are a function of its DAG node and lod. A rule that looks outside the brick would break the atlas silently; `ray-core.test.js` checks the property.
- **A chunk job is `expandChunk` with `seams: true`**, plus the buried-chunk shortcut in `createJobRunner`, computed by job-core's `fastChunk`. The runner is held to `expandChunk` on every chunk; keep shortcuts in the runner or `fastChunk`, not in `expandChunk`, so the dense oracles stay put. **A change to what `expandChunk` emits must reach `fastChunk` too**: `test/job-core.test.js` fails until it does. The masks rest on the lod-rule rule below (a brick's cells are a function of its node and lod); a rule that looked outside its brick would make the cache silently wrong.
- `dag-core.js` relies on **ids being interned children-first** (a child's id is always below its parent's). `createClasses` and `census` walk ids in order on the strength of it.
- **An attribute word's layout** is decided in `attr-core.js` only and decoded in `chunk-glsl.js`'s `GLSL_ATTR` only. Change the two together and run `chunk.test.js`. Reserved bits are written 0; a new field in them must bring its own coarse reduction (the colour's is the count-weighted mean) and its own oracle in `test/attrs.test.js`.
- **`attrAtCPU` / `hitAttrCPU` (`ray-core.js`) and `GLSL_RAY_ATTR` (`ray-glsl.js`) change together, line for line**, like the classify and trace twins. A page's attribute block belongs to the page, not the shared brick: whether a cell is shown depends on its neighbours.
- **A world file's layout is decided in `vdag-core.js` only.** Files outlive code: a change to the bytes needs a new version or a new section (readers skip tags they don't know), and the old bytes must still read, as `test/vdag-core.test.js`'s hand-written ones hold. Its golden hashes pin the canonical bytes of every kind of section: if one moves, the format moved. `test/fixtures/vdag-core-541d9d6.js` is the reader and writer before ATTR, frozen: never edit it. **A file is outside input**: the reader validates what it decodes, and anything from a file that reaches `innerHTML` goes through `esc()`. Adding a world kind needs its `colourOf` field to work from the descriptor and table alone, and a case in `file.test.js`.
- **Occlusion's decisions live in `occlusion-core.js`** (which chunk to skip, which to ask about, and the tile test `hiddenByTiles`); the page only draws the boxes or the tiles and moves the results (`queryRunner`, `depthRunner`). **A skip must never change a frame at rest**: `occlusion.test.js` holds it to the frame drawn without, byte for byte, so a change to the query pass (the pad, the depth state, the near test, what's a candidate) runs that suite. The bench's rows without `/occlusion` run with it off, so older benches stay comparable. `ray.test.js` benches the seven older rows only (all ten pass 15 min under SwiftShader); `occlusion.test.js` checks the occlusion pair.
- **Colour fields are named, or baked and shipped with the snapshot** (docs/glb-colour-plan.md §3, decided 2026-11-08).
  - A named field travels as a descriptor that `createJobRunner(snap, colourOf)` resolves.
  - A baked field (an imported asset's words for its skin) is data. It goes to the workers in the snapshot (`snap.colour.words`, cloned as the keys are), kept for the snapshot's life and handed to a restarted worker and the main-thread runner by the pool (glb-colour step 5, log §7). Each side builds the field over its own view: `colourOf(desc, { store, counts, D })`.
  - `editedField` wraps a field without its `mean`, so an edited baked world would walk a field that has no words off the skin. No baked world can be edited today (files are views); make the edited field keep `mean` before one can.
  - Either way, keep `stream-core.js` free of the colour imports.
- `docs/{hashcons,records,coverage,stream,ray,attrs,path-cost,labels,labels-kit,labels-mesh,lodacne}-results.md` are generated; the readings go in the matching `-findings.md` (`expand-findings.md` for records, `path-occupancy.md` for path-cost, `labels-log.md` for labels, `labels-kit-log.md` for labels-kit, `labels-mesh-log.md` for labels-mesh, `lodacne.md` for lodacne).
- **A new `lodRule` needs its formula written again in `ruleDownsample`** (`test/chunk-core.test.js`), the dense oracle, and a sparse case: dense content caps every per-brick rule at "keep all", so a mutant can hide there (`docs/coverage-log.md` §3). Per-brick rules decide per 8³ brick of coarse cells so the apron sees what the neighbour draws; keep them a function of the brick's node only.
- **`specimen-core.js` is a verbatim port.** Don't edit inside its slices: re-port from the sibling instead, and record new goldens only from builds that equal the sibling's.
- **`union`, `graft`, `importKeys` and `prune` (dag-core) keep ids children-first**, like every other builder; their tests check it. `prune` relies on it: it marks reachable nodes in one sweep down the ids.
- **The scatter build's decisions live in `scatter-core.js`**, shared by `buildScatter` (Node) and `scatter-build.js` (the page). `stream.test.js` pins the page's D = 9 world to Node's voxel count (2,916,229) and its pruned node count (35,295) since `CRUST` became 8 (17,596,293 and 36,368 at a crust of 64; 55,656,241 and 29,773 before the ground was a crust, 2026-11-13): a change to the layout, the recipes or the ground changes that number, so regenerate it from `buildScatter`.
- **The same for the lattice**: its decisions live in `lattice-core.js`, and `stream.test.js` pins the page's `11,6,5,4,3,1,random,0` lattice to Node's 24,412 voxels and 3,184 nodes (`buildLattice`, pruned, so the count doesn't depend on how the build was split). Changing `nodesPerR2` or `NODES_SHARED` needs `lattice-core.test.js`'s budget test to still hold the estimate above the real count (the worst cases are at the smallest n, docs/lattice3d-log.md §7), and the no-lost-room test to hold it at or below the old flat 0.4·R². The repeat bound holds **only when B divides the pitch** (`oneBlock`): elsewhere graft's unions make nodes it doesn't count. **No spreads (`...`) over a lattice's picks, prints or blocks**: at 128³ they're millions long.
- **The caves' decisions live in `cave-core.js`'s `cavePlan`**, and `voxelKind` is the rule every shortcut is held to. **THE CAP** rests on registering each primitive in every grid cell its bounding box touches; a primitive whose distance can exceed its box's distance by more than nothing (anything but a true lower bound outside the box) breaks it silently, and `test/cave-core.test.js` checks it against all primitives. **POOLS** rest on the box being the chamber's ellipsoids grown by WOBBLE + 1: a new carving primitive must enter the pool-level rule. `stream.test.js` pins the page's D = 10 caves (`caveDesc({ D: 10 })`) to Node's 386,153,834 voxels and 83,692 nodes (`buildCavesInParts`, pruned): a change to the heights, the plan or its draws changes them, so regenerate them.
- **The kit's decisions live in `kit-core.js`'s `kitPlan`**: the build and the colour field both read the plan, and the test stamps the world from it with its own arithmetic. `stream.test.js` pins the page's D = 10 kit (`kitDesc({ D: 10 })`) to Node's 76,250,785 voxels, 3,092 nodes and 290 Oh classes, and the same kit at seed 2 to 53,302,423 voxels and 3,430 nodes (seed 1's ornament library): a change to the masks, the parts, the families, the draws' order or the ground changes them, so regenerate them from `buildKit`. Draws are consumed in a fixed order (mask, skyline heights, then child and turn slot by slot); adding a draw anywhere moves every later choice.
- **The dungeon's decisions live in `dungeon-core.js`** (seeds, the collapse, the join, the tiles' voxels), and the walk in `test/dungeon-core.test.js` is what "all connected" means: a new tile needs its faces to be its sockets and the walk to still pass. `stream.test.js` pins the page's D = 9, 3-floor dungeon (seed 1) to Node's 8,656,926 voxels and 1,210 nodes, 4 floors to 11,615,466 and 1,263, seed 2 to 11,547,792 and 1,341 (`buildDungeon`, since the second round's tiles; 8,632,838 and 1,015 before): a change to the tiles, the weights, the seeds or the join changes them, so regenerate them, and the golden tables in the Node test.
- **The city's decisions live in `city-core.js`** (districts, lots, buildings, plans, tiles), and the walk in `test/city-core.test.js` is what "rooms can be entered" means: a new tile, furniture or plan rule needs the walk to still pass (two voxels clear round anything that isn't walked over). **A storey's codes may depend on its tier and parity only** (and a tier's first storey, a gabled house's last): `buildCity`'s period memo rests on it, and the DAG test catches a band that's wrong. `stream.test.js` pins the page's D = 9 city (seed 1) to Node's 5,423,681 voxels, 3,525 nodes, 14 buildings and 4,811 rooms, seed 2 to 5,472,702 and 3,401 (`buildCity`); `fileSelftest` pins seed 1 too: a change to the plan, the tiles or the furniture changes them, so regenerate them, and the golden prints in the Node test.
- **The same for clay**: `stream.test.js` pins the page's default clay (`clayDesc()`) to Node's 8,783,848 voxels and 12,985 nodes (`buildClay`), and flat ground at D = 20 to 20 nodes and 2⁴⁶ voxels. A change to the lump's place or size changes them, so regenerate them. **Coarse counts are float64**: a chunk at lod 12 or above can't feel a single voxel, so **never compare a count to 8^level to ask whether a node is full: ask `isFull`** (docs/edit-log.md §2, where that made a job differ from `expandChunk`). The per-brick rule may read rounded counts: that only makes near-equal cells tie, and ties are a function of the node.
- **`pick`'s rule is a positive length inside a half-open voxel**, stated so an oracle needs no walk order, and it divides rather than keeping `1/d`, so an exact tie is a tie. `test/edit-core.test.js` holds it in exact fractions. **A worker's colour table follows the log by epoch and count, never count alone**: undo then a different place leaves the same count with a different op on the end.
- **An edit is a transaction (stream-core §3): every frame draws one root, and `coverOf` knows nothing of edits.** A change that lets a result of an open edit reach the cache before its flip, or that dispatches anything else while an edit is open, breaks that. `frameChecker`'s `oneRoot` and the page's `invariants()` hold it.
- **A stamp is the seven nodes a job reads, and a word reads only inside its own chunk's box** (stream-core §2c). A job that reads anything else, a wider apron or a colour from a neighbour's box, makes the cache silently wrong: "same stamp, same bytes" in `test/stream-core.test.js` holds it, on every chunk. Change the stamp with the job.
- **Actors are order-free** (actor-core.js): anything about another actor is read from last tick's `old`, every random choice is `mix` of its reasons (never a stream), and the world is one `dagWorld` for the whole tick. The test shuffles the order every tick and wants the same bytes. **After an edit, hand the tick a new `dagWorld`**: the population drops rooms from any other world, and a room kept across an edit lets an actor through a new block (the `stale` mutant).
- **The aim reads the log's root, not the shown one** (edit mode): a second click before the flip acts on what the first left. The outline moves at once for the same reason. `editSelftest` holds it with two breaks and no frame between.
- **Editing is on `W.edit`, which only a world with a real store has** (clay, whose `tools` are 'blocks' and 'scene', and the kit, the shapes, the rows and files with a named colour, 'scene' alone: onelog-plan Q1), with one log for both kinds. The kit's real store is its pruned keys imported as themselves, checked at install: a renumbered store would make every tail name the wrong nodes. **A file's is the same import, and a file that doesn't import as itself (a node held twice) stays a view with no tool**: never give `newSceneEdit` a store whose ids aren't the snapshot's. The scatter, the lattice, the caves and large files make the same import at the first `B` (`makeReal`), and swap the streamer's store with `setStore`, never `setWorld` (which would flush). A world that takes none says why in `W.noEdit`; a new reason needs its case in `toolSelftest`. `fileExport` and `shelfAdd` are off while there are edits, because neither can hold their colours until §11.8 step 9. `W.root`, `W.voxels` and `W.nodes` follow the flip (`editFlipped`), not the click.
- **Hidden panels still get written**: `hud()` refreshes `__stream`, and tests read the HUD's text, so hiding must never stop it. **Anything asked for that only answers in a panel must open it** (`setHud(true)`): a bench, a file's error. `showPanels` is the only code that sets the panels' `hidden`, so the lock and the switch can't disagree.
- **The wireframe is a pass after `renderView`** (`drawWire`), so it can't change a frame the tests compare or what occlusion sees. The raster's vertex shader stays one string (`rasterVS('')`): `WIRE_VS` only adds a condition. `cubeIndex` holds the triangles, then the lines at an offset, because a chunk's VAO holds that binding. **The raster and the wireframe draw the first three faces' indices only** (18 triangles' worth, 30 lines): `rasterVS` picks which face of each axis, the one on the eye's side. The face it picks when the eye is inside a cube's slab is back-facing either way, so it rests on `CULL_FACE` in the raster pass (the wireframe drops back faces itself). A new shader drawing records through `rasterVS` must draw 18 indices; the preview's and the bodies' shaders turn their cubes and draw 36. A change to which faces a record shows must keep `quadsShown` equal to `shownMask`; `chunk-core.test.js` holds it on all 64 sets of open sides.
- **Actors draw in `renderView` only when there are some** (`actors.A && actors.n`): with none, the frame is today's call for call, and the near plane's line computes what it did. `actorSelftest` holds a frame after fliers come and go to the one before, byte for byte, raster only and ray only. Their pass sits after the occlusion queries (a flier must not hide a chunk from them) and before the ray pass (whose bound is the raster's depth, fliers included).
- **A body is turned in the shader only** (`BODY_VS`): one buffer a body, `uTurn` the transpose of body-core's row-major `turnMap`, `uShift` its offset less the turned box's corner plus the actor's place. `turnMap` and scatter-core's `rotateOcc` are one map (body-core.test), and `actorSelftest`'s turns check holds the pixels to `rotateOcc`, so a change to either side runs both.
- **Only an edit can trap an actor** (actor-core's `rescue`): ticks never enter rock and spawns check rock and sealed, so the page rescues only when the root moves under a population, and only actors within `reach` of `diffBox`. A new way to move an actor (a teleport, a turn that changes its box) must keep that, or call `rescue` itself.
- **A placement's two forms hold each other** (scene-core.js): `bakeScene` (into the DAG) and `compositeWorld` (each child asked in its own frame) share nothing but `boxHits` and `child()`, so a change to either runs `test/scene-core.test.js`, where both meet a dense stamp and fliers must move byte for byte on both. Offsets are whole voxels and turns are Oh elements: that's what keeps a box a box in every frame, so nothing is approximated. **Outside a child is the parent's space**; only the root's edge is solid.
- **A scene's colour is a field over its placements, not the ids** (scene-core's `sceneField`): a knight dropped into solid kit leaves the kit's node as it was. **Fields get the sample's level** as a fourth argument (`colour(x, y, z, level)`, the point being a level-`level` cell's centre; chunk-core's `meanUnder` and lod 0 pass it). Every field written before it takes three and ignores it; a field that reads it (the scene field, and any wrapper of one: `editedField` passes it on) must get it from everything that samples, or a coarse cell of a placed child reads one voxel. `test/scene-field.test.js` holds both sides.
- **A drop is an edit** (scene-core's log, stream-core's tail): `setRoot` with the log's paint, the drops in the tail's `scene`, after the keys they name. A drop carries T because a worker can't intern; a baked child's words cross once a world, so **a follower that loses a world list or a drop must throw**, never guess (the pool restarts it with every tail). Paint is bounds, never shape, so over-painting is the only error allowed. Blocks and drops in one world need one log to order them (scene-plan §4 Q3): **the one log** (log-core.js), whose delta stream-core carries as the tail's `ops`. The old roads still refuse each other; the page has used the one log alone since onelog-plan step 3. **"Latest" counts from op 0 of the log on both sides**: a follower numbers each op by its place in the log, never in its delta, or a block and a drop arriving in different deltas swap which is later (two mutants in one-log.test.js).
- **The mouse camera's decisions live in `camera-core.js`**; the page hands it deltas. It moves `cam.eye`, `cam.yaw` and `cam.pitch` and nothing else, so everything that reads the fly camera still does. **The target is kept only while nothing else moves the camera** (`orbitCam.seen` against `camKey()`): a new way to move the camera needs nothing, but a new way that moves it *as the orbit camera* must go through `camTo`, or the next gesture picks a new target. `camera.test.js` holds the page to the core run in the test file; a change to which button does what changes both.
- **Walking's decisions live in `walk-core.js`** (docs/dungeon-log.md §11): the page hands it the keys, the pad and the world, and puts the eye at `walkerEye`. Flying stays the default. The world is the log's root (as the actors' and the aim's) through `openWorld(withHoles(...))`, with the dungeon's secret doors as holes; a new kind of passable stone goes in the holes, never in the DAG. **The walker follows the eye**: anything else that moves `cam.eye` needs nothing, since the walker is put where the eye is. While walking, drags look (`cameraBy`) and nothing dollies; a new input goes in `walkStep` and `walk.test.js`, with a page mutant. The walker's sizes are dyadic on purpose: a box clamped at a voxel face must add back to an integer.
- **Nothing edit mode draws goes in `renderView`.** The outline, the ghost and the placement box are drawn on the canvas after the blit (`drawOutline`), so frames the tests compare byte for byte don't hold them.
- **The placement box's decisions live in `gizmo-core.js`**; the page passes rays and boxes and draws lines. **A drag is a function of where it started and the pointer's ray now** (`p.drag` is made once, at the press), and **nothing is baked or interned until Enter**: `sceneSelftest` holds the store's size across a box opened, changed and closed. The pointer's ray is `viewProj`'s arithmetic written three times: `pointerRay` and `screenOf` in the page, `rayOf` and `pageOf` in `scene.test.js`; change them with the field of view. A new handle or key needs its rule in the core's test (and a mutant), and its wiring in `boxInput` (and a page mutant).
- **The preview stays out of `renderView`** (`drawPreview`, after the frame and before the box's lines) and reads the raster's depth texture by hand (`rasterTMax`), so it needs the frame drawn at the same size first; a probe draws `renderView` into its target before it. It sorts its own cubes in the canvas's depth buffer, which nothing else uses (`depth: true` is asked for by name). `previewMap` (gizmo-core) and `PREVIEW_VS` are one map written twice: change them together, and run `test/gizmo-core.test.js` and `previewSelftest`. Its lod rule is *any*, because that's the bake's; the per-brick rules drop thin content (the fin in `previewChildren` is what tells them apart). **Which lod is drawn is `previewPlan`'s, and a lod is drawn only when all its chunks are made** (half a lod over the coarse one is two surfaces). The page's part is `previewNow` (moving: a handle held, or the placement or the camera changed in the last 200 ms) and `previewMake`; a record's chunk is a third word (`aChunk`, 10 bits an axis, which is why `PREVIEW_SPAN` is 16). `previewChunks` skips a chunk only if it's empty, or full with six full neighbours: the block in `previewChildren` holds its cubes to every chunk made one by one.
- **Both tools act on release**: a press may be a drag (a handle, or a look), told from a click by `CLICK_PX`; a block press held still past the repeat's first beat digs instead, and its release does nothing more. Neither takes a pointer lock, so `edit.pointer` is where they aim; null (no mouse seen yet, a selftest) is the middle of the view, with `fwd()` itself as the direction (the block tool makes a pointer's ray a unit, since a pick's t is its reach).
- **A world's snapshot and its tailer are made together**, or the tailer is made over a store imported from the snapshot's keys id for id (`makeReal`): what matters is that it starts where the snapshot ends, and `made real late` in `test/stream-core.test.js` holds a late tailer's tails to an install-time one's byte for byte. A job names its root. Tails reach a worker in order on the port its jobs use, so a job runs with exactly the nodes and colour table it was dispatched with: post a tail anywhere else and a job can read a table from its future.
- **`bakeDepth` (glb-core) is the one build of an asset's depth**, shared by `mesh-vdag.mjs` and `convert/`: shape, skin, the union check, words, in that order. Anything that interns between those steps moves the ids and so the file's bytes; checks go after it and only read. `test/convert-core.test.js` holds the page's bytes to the script's.

## Architecture notes

- The fragment shader is assembled from `GLSL_HEAD + GLSL_CLASSIFY + GLSL_TRACE + GLSL_SHADE`. The probes reuse the same pieces, so they test what the sheet runs.
- `uniformsOf(prog)` + `bindRow(U, rules, L, row)` bind a row for any program. Uniforms a program doesn't use come back `null`, which is harmless.
- `traceTower` carries the current cell as an integer across faces. Don't go back to re-deriving it from `floor(o + t·d)`: that stalls rays (`docs/tracer-cell-carry.md`).
- Public surface: `towerSelftest(opts)`, `towerSelftest.explain(entry)`, `window.__selftest`, `?selftest[=full]`, `?t=r4.<L>.<hex16>…` permalinks (v3's base-36 links are refused with a message).
